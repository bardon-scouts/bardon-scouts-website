Scouting is about learning by doing. At Bardon Scouts, members learn practical skills through hands-on activities at weekly meetings, on camps and at events, with help from leaders and older Scouts.

## What Scouts Learn

**Outdoor skills** are at the centre: camping and bushcraft, bushwalking and navigation, knots and rope work, lighting fires and cooking outdoors, building shelters and basic survival.

**Leadership and teamwork** come from working in patrols. Scouts plan and run activities, solve problems as a team, and older members mentor younger ones.

**Practical life skills** include first aid, thinking through risks, using and looking after tools, cooking, and caring for the environment.

**Personal development** runs through all of it: setting and reaching goals, becoming more independent, sticking with things that are hard, community service, and respect for other cultures.

## How Skills Grow Through the Sections

Skills build as members move up through the sections. [Joeys](/sections/joeys/) start with outdoor play, simple knots and team games. [Cubs](/sections/cubs/) learn camping basics, knots, outdoor cooking and an introduction to navigation. [Scouts](/sections/scouts/) move on to more advanced camping, expeditions and leading their patrol. [Venturers](/sections/venturers/) plan their own expeditions, take on complex projects and mentor younger members.

## Outdoor Adventure Skills and Badges

Scouts Australia's **Outdoor Adventure Skills** (OAS) framework sets out progressive stages in bushcraft, bushwalking, camping, and navigation and orienteering. Scouts work through the stages in each area, building on what they already know.

All badge work is recorded in **Scouts | Terrain**. As well as Outdoor Adventure Skills, badge areas include STEM & Innovation, Creative Arts, Growth & Development, and Creating a Better World. See our [Terrain](/terrain/) page for how parents can follow their child's progress.

## How Skills Are Taught at Bardon

Weekly meetings include skill activities, games and challenges set for each section. Camps give members time to practise outdoor skills properly. Events like [Operation NightHawk](/operation-nighthawk/), [KnightMoves](/knightmoves/) and [Skillorama](/skillorama/) test what they've learned. Older Scouts teach younger ones, which builds their own skills and leadership too.

Parents can help by encouraging practice at home (knots and outdoor cooking are good places to start), asking what your child is learning, helping them set goals in Terrain, and seeing skills in action on your parent roster night.
